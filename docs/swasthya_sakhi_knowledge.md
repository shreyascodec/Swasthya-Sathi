# Swasthya Sakhi — Knowledge (maternal & child health screening)

Reference for the PAL. This kiosk **screens and refers; it does not diagnose or
prescribe.** ⚠️ POC placeholder values — **pending clinician review** (align to
MoHFW / PMSMA / WHO before clinical use).

## What Swasthya Sakhi does
Reads a pregnant woman's ANC card / lab report, checks a few key values, asks
antenatal danger-sign questions, and gives a **risk level + next step**. Spoken
lines come from the kiosk in **Hindi or English** (patient's choice); this
document is background only.

## Antenatal danger signs (any "yes" = urgent — advise immediate referral)
- Severe headache, blurred vision, or spots before the eyes
- Any vaginal bleeding
- Reduced or absent fetal (baby's) movement
- Sudden or severe swelling of face, hands, or feet
- Fever or chills
- (Also urgent if mentioned: convulsions/fits, severe abdominal pain, difficulty breathing,
  leaking of fluid before term)

## Key values and what they mean (screening cut-offs, not diagnosis)
- **Blood pressure:** ≥140/90 = raised; **≥160/110 = severe** (pre-eclampsia risk)
- **Haemoglobin:** <11 g/dL = anaemia; **<7 g/dL = severe anaemia**
- **Blood sugar (GDM):** fasting ≥92 mg/dL, or 2-hour 75g ≥140 mg/dL = high; ≥200 = very high
- **Urine protein:** present ("+") with raised BP = concerning (pre-eclampsia)
- **Temperature:** ≥37.5 °C = raised (possible infection)

## Risk levels and the next step
- **Low risk** — values in range, no danger signs → continue routine ANC; keep taking
  IFA/calcium; attend the next scheduled visit.
- **Needs attention** — a raised value but nothing critical → see the Medical Officer soon;
  a follow-up ANC visit is advised; monitor the flagged values.
- **High risk** — any severe value (e.g., BP ≥160/110, Hb <7) **or any reported danger sign**
  → go to the First Referral Unit (FRU)/CHC now; **call 108** if a danger sign is present.

## How to speak to the patient
- Warm, simple, in her language; short sentences; reassure, don't alarm.
- Name a flagged value plainly ("your blood pressure is a little high") — never label a disease.
- Never give medicine names or doses. Never promise outcomes.
- Always end a concerning result with a clear, doable next step and who to see.
